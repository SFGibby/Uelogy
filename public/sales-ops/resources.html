Concert Finance / Propel

Underwriting Information

FICO Requirement: 660+ (680 in TX & FL)
Credit approval is valid 180 days from approval date
DTI is not considered
Co-Applicants must reside on the property

2nd Systems and Home Types

2nd systems will only be approved if it has its own components, gateway, POI, and monitoring. It may not share ANY components with the existing system. (CA and HI only)

Approved Home Types: Multi-family, rental/vacation property, mobile home with permanent foundation.

NTP Requirements

The contract must be signed. System design is submitted in the portal and reviewed in the Greentech portal. Once system design is reviewed and approved, NTP will be granted and the project can move toward installation.

Welcome Call and Stips

A welcome call won't be required. Stipulations: Photo ID and Verification of Income. If the property is a Mobile Home, Title Verification will be required as well.

Early Buyout Option

At year 5, 6.5, and year 8; the customer has the option to purchase the system, or terminate the ESA. The 3rd party owner will provide limited warranty and asset management services until the end of year 25, if the EBO is exercised. If they choose NOT to exercise the EBO, at the end of the 25 year loan:
  • They can purchase the system for fair market value.
  • They can renew the agreement for 10 additional one-year terms.
  • They can have the system removed at their expense.

What is Propel?

Propel pairs an Energy Services Agreement with a loan to provide a predictable monthly payment with an Early Buyout Option (EBO).

Underproduction

The ESA includes a 5-year production guarantee. Customer will receive a refund if the system doesn't meet production.

Home Sale

In the event of a home sale, the loan must be transferred, paid off, or included in the sale of the home. After year 5, the loan must be paid off. ESA cannot be transferred.

What is PayKeeper?

PayKeeper's Automated Escrow protects both the homeowner's payment and the project. Escrow is a neutral third party that holds funds securely until agreed-upon conditions are met, so the contractor can begin work with confidence they'll be paid on time, and the homeowner can relax knowing their money is safe until the work is done.

How It Works

  1. Create and Fund Your Account: The homeowner's escrow account is held in their name and is FDIC-insured up to $250,000.
  2. Payouts as Milestones Are Met: PayKeeper verifies milestone completion before payments are sent to the contractor.
  3. Homeowner Approves Final Payout: Once the homeowner is satisfied and approves the last milestone, the final payment is released to the contractor.

FDIC Insurance & Licensing

Funds are FDIC-insured up to $250,000. PayKeeper is a Licensed Escrow Agent, NMLS #2401821.

Adders Guide

What each adder is, why it's needed, and what's included. No pricing here; for current pricing, see the official adder sheet from Sales Ops.

Roof & StructuralElectricalTrenching & Site AccessTravelBatteriesPanels & EquipmentSmart HomeOther
Roof & Structural
Roof & Structural
▾

Roof Pitch ≥ 36° (9/12 pitch)

Why it's needed: Steep roofs require additional fall-protection gear and slow the crew down significantly.
What's included: Extra harnesses/roof jacks and the added labor time for a safe, steep-roof install.

Critter Guard

Why it's needed: Open space under panels invites birds and rodents, which can chew wiring and cause damage or fire risk over time.
What's included: Mesh guard material and installation around the full perimeter of the array.

Metal Roof

Why it's needed: Metal roofing requires different mounting hardware and flashing than standard shingle roofs to avoid leaks and maintain warranty.
What's included: Metal-roof-specific mounts, sealing, and the extra labor for that install method.

Tile Roof

Why it's needed: Tile is fragile and requires careful removal/replacement plus specialized flashing (tile hooks) to avoid cracking tiles during install.
What's included: Tile hooks/flashing and the added time this install method takes.

Flat Roof Ballast

Why it's needed: Flat roofs have no pitch to rack panels against gravity, so the system needs to be weighted down instead of angled and bolted like a pitched roof.
What's included: Ballasted (weighted) tilt-racking system so panels don't need roof penetrations for support.

Ground Mount (More or Less than 6kW)

Why it's needed: Ground mounts need their own foundation, dedicated racking, and a trenched conduit run back to the home, none of which a roof mount requires.
What's included: Foundation work, ground-mount racking, and the conduit run. Smaller systems (under 6kW) carry a higher per-watt rate since foundation/setup costs don't scale down with system size.

Site Survey

Why it's needed: Some sites need a dedicated in-person technician visit beyond the standard remote/virtual assessment to confirm the project is feasible as designed.
What's included: A technician site visit and the resulting documentation used to finalize design.

Multiple Planes (every plane over 3)

Why it's needed: Each additional roof plane needs its own design pass, mounting layout, and wiring run; it isn't just "more of the same."
What's included: The added engineering and installation time for each plane beyond the third.

Small System (under 4.5kW / under 3kW)

Why it's needed: Very small systems still carry the same fixed costs (permitting, crew mobilization, a site visit) that a larger system spreads across more watts.
What's included: Covers those fixed project costs that a small system otherwise can't absorb on its own. Systems under 3kW also require Ops approval before they can be sold.

Reroof

Why it's needed: If the existing roof needs to be replaced before solar can go on it, that work has to happen first and is scoped separately.
What's included: Custom quoted based on the roofer's assessment of what the reroof requires.

Structural Upgrades

Why it's needed: If the roof or attic framing can't support the added weight of the system, it needs to be reinforced to meet code and keep the install safe.
What's included: Custom quoted based on the structural engineer's assessment.

Smoke Detectors

Why it's needed: Some jurisdictions require code-compliant smoke detectors to be installed or updated as a condition of the solar permit.
What's included: Included at no separate charge to the customer when the AHJ requires it.
Electrical
Electrical
▾

⚠️ Before you add an electrical adder on a TPO deal

On Loan and Cash deals, an electrical adder that turns out to be unneeded can simply be reallocated back to commission: quick and painless.

On TPO deals (Lease/PPA), the financier underwrites and funds based on the full scope submitted. If an adder like an MPU isn't actually needed, it takes a formal change order to remove it, which is slow and a poor customer experience.

Rule of thumb: don't add a "just in case" electrical adder to a TPO deal unless you're highly confident it's needed. If there's real uncertainty, submit without it and let Operations' site review confirm whether it's required before install.

MPU Avoidance (PCS)

Why it's needed: A Power Control System lets the home safely add solar without needing a full Main Panel Upgrade.
What's included: The PCS device and the labor to install it in place of a panel replacement.

Main Breaker Derate

Why it's needed: Some panels can legally accommodate solar without a full upgrade if the main breaker is "derated" per code (NEC 120% rule).
What's included: The engineering assessment and labor to perform the derate.

Relocate

Why it's needed: Sometimes the electrical panel needs to physically move to make room for new solar equipment or meet code clearances.
What's included: Labor and materials to relocate the panel.

Subpanel (incl. Colorado)

Why it's needed: Added when the existing panel doesn't have room (breaker slots) for the new solar circuits.
What's included: The subpanel itself and the wiring/labor to tie it in. Colorado has its own line item due to local code/labor differences.

AC Relocate (<10ft)

Why it's needed: The AC disconnect or related equipment sometimes needs to move a short distance for code clearance or space reasons.
What's included: Relocation labor for moves under 10 feet.

Meter Main Combo

Why it's needed: Combines the meter and main panel into a single unit when the existing setup can't support a clean solar interconnection.
What's included: The combo panel/meter unit and its installation.

Panel Upgrade/Replacement

Why it's needed: The home's main service panel doesn't have the capacity, or is too outdated, to safely support the new solar system.
What's included: A new panel and all associated labor to bring the service up to code.

Hawaiian Tie-In

Why it's needed: A "Hawaiian Tie-in" is an electrical technique used to connect solar systems to older homes without costly main panel upgrades. It involves moving existing household load breakers from the main panel into a newly added subpanel. This clears the main panel's busbar so the solar inverter can connect directly to it.
What's included: The specialized tie-in hardware and labor required.

Full Service Upgrade

Why it's needed: The entire electrical service (panel, meter, and service entrance) needs upgrading to support the system, beyond what a simple panel swap covers.
What's included: The complete service upgrade, materials and labor.

EV Combiner Box

Why it's needed: Lets an EV charger circuit be safely added alongside the solar system's electrical scope.
What's included: The combiner box and associated wiring.

EV Charger

Why it's needed: Added when the customer wants a home EV charger installed as part of their project.
What's included: The charger unit and its installation.
Trenching & Site Access
Trenching & Site Access
▾

Dirt < 50 Feet

Why it's needed: Underground conduit is required to run power between a ground mount, detached structure, or subpanel and the home.
What's included: Trenching and conduit placement in dirt for runs up to 50 feet.

Dirt > 50 Feet

Why it's needed: Same as above, just a longer run; longer trenches take proportionally more crew time and equipment.
What's included: A fixed base cost for the setup plus an incremental per-foot cost for footage beyond 50 feet.

Grass/Gravel/Rock

Why it's needed: These surfaces are harder to trench through than plain dirt and may need different equipment or restoration after the work.
What's included: Custom quoted based on the specific terrain and scope.

Asphalt/Concrete

Why it's needed: Cutting through hardscape requires saw-cutting and patching/repair afterward, which dirt trenching doesn't.
What's included: Custom quoted based on the cutting and patch-repair scope.

What's the difference between a lease and a PPA?
Financing & Products
▾
Both are TPO (third-party-owned) products: a financier owns the system, not the homeowner. A lease charges a fixed monthly payment for use of the system regardless of how much it actually produces. A PPA charges the homeowner per kWh the system actually produces, so the bill scales up or down with production. Neither one makes the homeowner eligible for the federal tax credit, since they don't own the system.
What is Concert Propel?
Financing & Products
▾
Propel pairs an Energy Services Agreement (ESA) with a loan to give the homeowner one predictable monthly payment, plus an Early Buyout Option (EBO). See the Finance Resource Guide's Concert one-pager for full underwriting details.
What is an Early Buyout Option (EBO)?
Financing & Products
▾
An EBO lets the customer buy out the system at set milestones in the contract (for example, years 5, 6.5, and 8 on Concert Propel) instead of continuing payments through the full term.
Why do some financiers cap the monthly payment instead of the system price?
Financing & Products
▾
Financiers like LightReach underwrite around affordability, not just system cost, so instead of a hard dollar cap on system price, they cap what the monthly payment can be (e.g., LightReach caps around $500/month) regardless of system size.
What is "Domestic Content" and why does it matter to a deal?
Domestic Content & Incentives
▾
Domestic Content refers to panels (and other equipment) manufactured to meet U.S. sourcing requirements. Systems built with qualifying Domestic Content equipment can be eligible for an additional boost on top of the base federal tax credit. This only matters for TPO systems, since loan/cash customers no longer receive a tax credit.
Does a lease or PPA customer get the federal tax credit?
Domestic Content & Incentives
▾
No. In a TPO deal (lease or PPA), the financier owns the system and is the one eligible to claim any tax credit, not the homeowner.
Do all battery/inverter combinations qualify for the Domestic Content boost?
Domestic Content & Incentives
▾
No, it depends on the equipment pairing. For example, pairing a Tesla Powerwall 3 with its own built-in inverter is not eligible for the Panel Domestic Content boost (though the Storage boost still applies), while pairing PW3 with Enphase microinverters keeps both the Panel and Battery Domestic Content boosts intact.
Which panels currently qualify as Domestic Content?

Why can't we just remove an unused MPU adder on a TPO deal the way we do on loan/cash?
TPO vs. Loan/Cash
▾
On loan/cash deals, there's no financier funding the project, so an adder that turns out to be unneeded can simply be reallocated back to commission. TPO deals are underwritten and funded based on the full scope submitted, so removing scope after the fact requires a formal change order to the financier, which is slower and creates friction.
I'm not sure if a TPO deal needs an electrical upgrade. What should I do?
TPO vs. Loan/Cash
▾
Don't add it "just in case." Submit the deal on TPO financing without the adder, and let Operations' standard site review confirm whether it's actually required. It's far less painful to add a confirmed requirement afterward (via change order, before install) than to try to remove an unconfirmed one after the fact.
